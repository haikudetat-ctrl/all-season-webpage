Why Not Build-Only

We can do build-only, but I do not think that is the best business move here.
The value is not just launching a site. The value is the first 90 days after launch, when we learn which sources contact, which ones set, which ones demo, and which ones produce jobs at a cost that makes sense.
If we build it and walk away, you have a better asset. If we build it and operate it, you have a cleaner revenue system.

Why Not Tool Replacement First

I would not start by ripping out LeadMaster, Hatch, CallTools, or whatever dialer you choose.
You already have pieces that can work. The failure point is the handoff. High-intent leads need to come in with context, get routed to the right queue, and be handled like someone who just raised their hand, not someone on a cold list.
Once that layer is working, we will know which tool limitations are real and which ones were process problems.

  • The old in-house handling benchmark was strong: roughly 70% contact and 45% set on high-intent web leads.
  • Roofing CAC has to be managed by sold job, not CPL.
  • Solar lease commoditization makes trust, roof expertise, and follow-up more important.
  • A rebrand-ready owned website increases enterprise value more than another rented lead source.

Objection Handling

"Why not just build the site?"

We can. That is Option B. But the problem you described is not only a site problem. It is source quality, first-touch handling, and attribution. Build-only gives you the asset. Option A gives you the asset plus the operating discipline to prove what it is worth.

"We already have a CRM and follow-up tools."

Good. I am not trying to replace them first. I am trying to make the lead context cleaner before it hits those tools, and make the outcome reporting cleaner after the team works it.

"Can you guarantee leads or sales?"

No one should guarantee that honestly. Platform cost, market behavior, and rep execution are real variables. What we guarantee is the build, the routing logic, the tracking visibility, and the operating cadence. You will know what happened and what decision to make next.

"Why pay monthly?"

Because the first version will not be the final answer. The money is made in the weekly tightening: which ad angle works, which form step drops people, which source sets, which script gets replies, which lead source should get cut.

"What happens if it does not work?"

After the first 90-day operating cycle, either side can end the retainer with 30 days notice. That gives the system enough time to prove something without locking you into a long-term commitment.
